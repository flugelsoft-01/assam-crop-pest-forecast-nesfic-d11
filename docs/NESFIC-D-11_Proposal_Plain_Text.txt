=== 1. Problem understanding & user workflow (307/500 words) ===
Agriculture in Assam supports over 70% of the state's population across diverse agro-climatic zones, spanning Sali and Boro paddy, tea, mustard, potato, maize, and horticultural crops. Currently, crop stress, nutrient deficiencies, and pest or disease outbreaks are detected largely through reactive, manual field scouting by farmers and Block/District Agriculture and Horticulture field staff. Because Assam's high-humidity monsoon and post-monsoon microclimates accelerate pathogen and pest cycles—such as Rice Blast (Magnaporthe oryzae), Brown Planthopper, Tea Mosquito Bug (Helopeltis), Mustard Aphid, and Potato Late Blight—visual symptoms often appear only after exponential spread has already caused irreversible yield loss.

The Assam State Space Application Centre (ASSAC) and the Department of Agriculture have identified three critical operational gaps. First, satellite observations alone lack the spatial granularity (sub-meter) and cloud-free continuity needed to pinpoint early canopy stress at the individual plot or cluster level, whereas standalone UAV/drone flights lack continuous state-wide temporal coverage. Second, existing pest/disease forecasting models cover only a narrow subset of pests, lack deeper epidemiological integration with real-time weather and crop-stage phenology, and remain disconnected from last-mile farmer workflows. Third, field extension workers lack a unified triage mechanism to distinguish current abiotic/nutrient stress from emerging biotic pest risk, resulting in delayed interventions or indiscriminate blanket chemical spraying that raises farmer input costs and ecological harm.

The intended users and beneficiaries are smallholder farmers, Agriculture/Horticulture field extension workers, Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) specialists, ASSAC geospatial analysts, and District Agriculture Officers (DAOs). Under the current workflow, a farmer notices severe leaf damage, reports it informally, an extension officer visits days later, and generic advisories are issued too late to contain the outbreak.

Our intended outcome is a shift from reactive firefighting to proactive, 6-to-12-day pre-symptomatic intervention: fusing repeated satellite passes, targeted UAV surveys, epidemiological forecasting, and field-officer verification into location-specific, multilingual advisories that cut avoidable crop loss and reduce unnecessary chemical inputs.

=== 2. Proposed solution & key features (301/500 words) ===
We propose AgriDrishti Assam, an end-to-end, multi-modal crop health intelligence and pest/disease forecasting platform purpose-built for ASSAC and the Department of Agriculture, compatible with all major agricultural and horticultural crops in Assam.

Instead of relying on a single data source or crop-specific silo, AgriDrishti Assam integrates four tightly coupled modules:

1. Multi-Modal Spatial & Spectral Fusion Engine: Continuously ingests public/departmental satellite imagery (Sentinel-2 NDVI, NDRE, NDWI) to flag block-level vegetation anomalies against crop-calendar phenological baselines. When cloud cover or moderate anomalies occur, the system triggers targeted UAV/drone RGB and multispectral orthomosaic surveys (2.5–3.5 cm/px GSD) to geolocate exact stress clusters and thermal anomalies.

2. Epidemiological Pest & Disease Forecasting Engine: Combines crop-stage susceptibility, degree-day accumulation, relative humidity, rainfall, and leaf-wetness duration with spectral/UAV stress indicators to generate 14-day forward risk trajectories (0–100 score, Low to Critical). Crucially, it separates current crop stress and nutrient deficiency (e.g., NDRE chlorophyll deficit without pathogen-favorable wetness) from emerging biotic pest/disease risk, providing calibrated confidence scores and interpretable driver attributions (e.g., '89% RH + 12.5h leaf wetness + -0.18 NDVI anomaly').

3. Human-in-the-Loop Field Verification & Audit Workflow: Flagged hotspots enter a prioritized triage queue for Agriculture/Horticulture field staff. Officers inspect geolocated clusters via mobile/web, log ground-truth observations or leaf photos, and either verify the threat, request a targeted UAV re-flight, or mark false positives (such as harvesting or cloud shadows). Every state transition is recorded in an immutable audit trail.

4. Multilingual IPM-First Advisory Studio: Once verified, the platform generates farmer-friendly, Integrated Pest Management (IPM) advisories in Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, and English, prioritizing cultural and biocontrol steps before threshold-based targeted spraying, ready for SMS, WhatsApp, or IVR broadcast.

Versus the current fragmented approach, AgriDrishti Assam delivers unified multi-crop coverage, 6–12 days earlier warning, explainable alerts, and closed-loop accountability between ASSAC analysts, extension workers, and farmers.

=== 3. Describe User Journey,Wireframes, Prototype & Proof of Work (273/500 words) ===
AgriDrishti Assam's user journey connects four operational personas in a closed loop:

1. ASSAC / Geospatial Analyst: Monitors the Assam Multi-Block Geospatial Risk Map, where districts and blocks are color-coded by composite risk. Clicking any block (e.g., Titabor Sali Paddy or Mandia Potato) opens the 4-week Sentinel-2 NDVI/NDRE trajectory alongside the 8x8 UAV orthomosaic canopy-stress and thermal-anomaly grid.

2. Epidemiological Forecaster / KVK Specialist: Reviews the 14-day SEIR-style pest risk curve with uncertainty bands and interpretable driver bars (Weather, UAV, Spectral, Phenology), adjusting microclimate sensitivity or validating threshold triggers.

3. Agriculture / Horticulture Field Officer: Receives prioritized alerts in the Field-Verification & Triage Queue. The officer inspects the geolocated cluster, enters field scouting notes, and clicks 'Verify Ground Truth', 'Request UAV Re-flight', 'Dispatch Advisory', or 'Mark False Positive'. Each action appends a timestamped, role-attributed entry to the Auditable Observation & Advisory Log.

4. Smallholder Farmer: Receives a concise, location-specific SMS/WhatsApp/IVR advisory in Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, or English detailing the exact block, crop threat, inspection checklist, and IPM-first action steps.

Proof of Work & Prototype Evidence: We have developed a working interactive web MVP (AgriDrishti Assam) demonstrating all four workflows end-to-end across six Assam blocks and six crop types (Sali Paddy, Tea, Mustard, Potato, Maize, and Banana). The accompanying Proof of Work PDF includes: (a) annotated UI screenshots/wireframes of the Spatial & Forecast Command, UAV Orthomosaic Grid, Verification Queue, and Multilingual Advisory Studio; (b) live simulation outputs of the spectral stress and epidemiological scoring engine showing how humidity, leaf wetness, and UAV stress dynamically shift 14-day risk trajectories; (c) multi-crop validation matrix results; and (d) localized advisory previews in Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, and English.

=== 4. Technology & solution architecture (262/500 words) ===
AgriDrishti Assam uses a modular, cloud-native, open-standards geospatial and AI architecture designed for interoperability with ASSAC GIS infrastructure and state agriculture portals:

1. Frontend & Field Interface: Built with React 18, TypeScript, Vite, and Tailwind CSS for responsive desktop command centers and low-bandwidth mobile field-worker browsers, supporting offline-first caching of verification forms and localized advisories.

2. Geospatial & Data Ingestion Layer: Python-based ingestion pipelines (FastAPI, Rasterio, GDAL, GeoPandas) process Sentinel-2 Level-2A multispectral bands (B04, B05, B08, B11) to compute NDVI, NDRE, and NDWI, applying cloud-masking (SCL/QA60) and OGC-compliant GeoJSON/COG (Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF) tiling. UAV RGB/multispectral orthomosaics (generated via OpenDroneMap/Pix4D outputs) are ingested as georeferenced tiles and summarized into sub-hectare stress polygons.

3. AI/ML & Epidemiological Modeling Core: Combines two complementary layers: (a) a spatio-temporal anomaly detector comparing rolling spectral indices against block-level phenological baselines and crop calendars; and (b) a hybrid mechanistic-ML epidemiological model integrating degree-day thermal suitability, relative humidity, rainfall, and leaf-wetness duration (calibrated to Assam Agricultural University and NIBSM pest-biology thresholds) with gradient-boosted/SEIR risk projection and SHAP-style feature attribution. Optional lightweight CNN/MobileNetV3 leaf-image classification supports field-officer photo verification.

4. Workflow, Audit & Advisory Services: PostgreSQL + PostGIS stores block polygons, observation time-series, and an append-only audit log of every alert, confidence score, officer verification, and dispatched advisory. A template + NLG localization engine renders advisories in Assamese, Bengali, Bodo, and English, exposing REST/webhook APIs for SMS gateways, WhatsApp Business, and IVR.

5. Hosting & Deployment: Containerized (Docker) microservices deployable on NIC MeghRaj / State Data Centre or sovereign Indian cloud regions, with role-based access control (RBAC) and TLS 1.3 encryption.

=== 5. Development methodology & intermediate steps (251/500 words) ===
We follow an iterative, milestone-gated Agile-GeoAI methodology co-designed with ASSAC and Department of Agriculture stakeholders across five structured phases:

Step 1 — Discovery, Crop-Calendar & Epidemiology Mapping: Conduct joint workshops with ASSAC, Department of Agriculture, Assam Agricultural University (AAU), and KVKs to finalize priority districts/blocks, crop calendars across Kharif, Rabi, and perennial crops, and historical pest/disease epidemiology parameters. Output: Signed-off Data Dictionary, Pest-Weather Threshold Matrix, and RBAC workflow spec.

Step 2 — Data Pipeline & Baseline Calibration: Build automated ingestion for Sentinel-2 imagery, IMD/AWS weather grids, and UAV orthomosaic tiles. Establish historical 3-to-5-year spectral phenological baselines per agro-climatic zone to distinguish normal senescence/harvesting from true stress. Output: Automated GeoTIFF/PostGIS pipeline and calibrated baseline curves.

Step 3 — Prototype & Epidemiological Model Development: Train and calibrate multi-crop anomaly detection and 14-day pest risk forecasting models using historical outbreak records and synthetic augmentation where ground labels are sparse. Integrate interpretable driver attributions and confidence scoring. Output: Functional API + interactive command prototype (demonstrated in current MVP).

Step 4 — Field-Verification App & Multilingual Advisory Integration: Deploy the mobile-friendly verification queue for extension officers with offline sync, geo-tagged photo capture, and immutable audit logging, paired with Assamese/Bengali/Bodo/English IPM advisory templates validated by AAU/NIBSM agronomists. Output: End-to-end MVP ready for controlled block trials.

Step 5 — Shadow-Mode Testing, Refinement & Handover: Run shadow-mode validation during an active cropping window, comparing model alerts against ground scouting to tune false-positive suppression (Smaller than 10% target), refine confidence thresholds, and finalize SOPs and API integration for state-wide scaling.

=== 7. Data & information requirements (246/500 words) ===
AgriDrishti Assam is designed with a clear separation between confirmed public/open datasets (usable immediately) and departmental datasets (accessed only subject to formal Government of Assam permissions during prototype/MVP phases):

1. Publicly Available Baseline Data (Confirmed Access): Sentinel-2 Level-2A multispectral imagery (10m/20m resolution, 5-day revisit via Copernicus/Earth Search STAC), NASA/IMERG or IMD open gridded meteorological data, SRTM/CartoDEM elevation layers, and published ICAR/AAU crop calendars and IPM package-of-practices.

2. Departmental & Institutional Data (Subject to Policy & Permissions): ASSAC agricultural GIS layers (block/village/plot boundaries, crop-sown area masks), scheduled UAV/drone RGB and multispectral orthomosaics (GeoTIFF, 2–5 cm GSD) with flight metadata, historical pest/disease incidence registers from the Department of Agriculture/ARIAS Society, and local Automatic Weather Station (AWS) feeds for finer microclimate resolution.

3. Formats & Processing Needs: Raster inputs in Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF (COG) / GeoTIFF (WGS84 / UTM 46N); vector boundaries in GeoJSON/Shapefile; weather and pest logs in JSON/CSV/REST APIs. Processing includes radiometric/atmospheric normalization, cloud/shadow masking, NDVI/NDRE/NDWI band math, and spatial aggregation to block/cluster polygons.

4. Annotation & Ground-Truth Requirements: Geo-tagged field scouting records (crop, growth stage, pest/disease name, severity score 1–5, leaf photo) collected via the extension officer app to continuously calibrate model thresholds.

5. Assumptions & Transparency: At the application stage, no unconfirmed departmental data access is assumed; all current prototype demonstrations use clearly labeled synthetic/public-calibrated data. If departmental UAV flights or AWS feeds are delayed in specific blocks, the platform gracefully degrades to Sentinel-2 + gridded weather + field-officer photo inputs with adjusted confidence scores.

=== 10. Security, privacy, safety & compliance (243/500 words) ===
Operating within Government of Assam departments requires strict adherence to data sovereignty, privacy, drone safety, and human-oversight standards:

1. Data Sovereignty & Access Security: All departmental GIS layers, farmer contact lists, and field observations are hosted within Indian sovereign cloud regions (NIC MeghRaj / State Data Centre compatible). Data in transit uses TLS 1.3; data at rest uses AES-256 encryption. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) enforces least-privilege separation between ASSAC geospatial analysts, District Agriculture Officers, block field staff, and system administrators.

2. Farmer Privacy & Consent: Farmer phone numbers and plot coordinates are treated as sensitive personal/livelihood data under India's Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act principles. Advisory broadcasts use opt-in/departmental registry IDs with pseudonymized analytics so aggregated district dashboards never expose individual farmer PII.

3. UAV / Drone Regulatory Safety: All UAV flights follow DGCA Drone Rules 2021 (UIN registration, Digital Sky NPNT/green-zone clearances, certified remote pilots, and maximum 120m AGL ceiling) with strict prohibition of flights over restricted zones.

4. Agricultural Safety & Human-in-the-Loop Governance: Automated AI alerts never dispatch chemical-spray recommendations directly to farmers without human review. Every high/critical alert requires field-officer verification or DAO sign-off, and advisories strictly follow CIB&RC-approved dosages and ICAR/AAU IPM-first guidelines to prevent misuse of banned or excessive pesticides.

5. Interoperability & Auditability: Open OGC/REST standards ensure seamless integration with ASSAC GIS and existing state portals. An append-only audit trail logs every model prediction, confidence score, officer verification note, and dispatched advisory for full departmental accountability.

=== 11. Innovation & differentiation (224/500 words) ===
Existing agricultural monitoring tools typically fall into two disconnected categories: macro-scale satellite NDVI dashboards (which miss early sub-plot pest outbreaks and suffer under Assam's heavy cloud cover) or isolated smartphone leaf-photo apps (which detect disease only after visible damage has occurred and lack epidemiological forecasting).

AgriDrishti Assam introduces three core innovations:

1. Diagnostic Disambiguation (Current Stress vs. Emerging Risk vs. Nutrient Deficit): By fusing Sentinel-2 red-edge/moisture indices (NDRE, NDWI), 3 cm/px UAV thermal-canopy anomalies, and microclimate leaf-wetness models, the platform explicitly distinguishes abiotic/nutrient stress from biotic pest/disease emergence—preventing unnecessary fungicide/pesticide use.

2. Hybrid Mechanistic-ML Epidemiology for All Assam Crops: Rather than black-box correlations that fail on new outbreaks, we embed scientific pest-biology rules (degree-days, humidity-wetness infection windows, phenological susceptibility) with ML anomaly detection, yielding interpretable risk drivers and 6–12 day early-warning lead times across Paddy, Tea, Mustard, Potato, Maize, and Horticulture.

3. Closed-Loop, Auditable Extension Workflow: Unlike one-way advisory portals, every alert carries calibrated confidence, triggers targeted UAV or field-officer verification, and feeds ground-truth back into model calibration while generating Assamese, Bengali, and Bodo IPM advisories.

IP & Dependencies: The multi-modal fusion scoring engine, epidemiological rule-calibration framework, and verification-audit schema represent our core proprietary IP (deployable under Government of Assam licensing). We leverage proven open-source foundations—React, TypeScript, FastAPI, GDAL/Rasterio, PostGIS, and public Copernicus Sentinel-2 data—eliminating vendor lock-in and keeping recurring licensing costs near zero.

=== 12. Resources, risks & pilot validation (229/500 words) ===
Indicative Resource & Cost Drivers: Major development and pilot validation cost drivers over 24 weeks include: (a) engineering & agricultural epidemiology manpower (~60% of budget); (b) sovereign cloud compute, PostGIS storage, and SMS/WhatsApp/IVR gateway credits (~15%); (c) targeted multispectral UAV survey sorties and field-scouting kits across pilot blocks (~15%); and (d) AAU/KVK validation workshops and multilingual extension training (~10%). Using open-source GeoAI stacks and public Sentinel-2 imagery avoids costly commercial satellite/software licenses.

Key Risks & Mitigations:
- Cloud Cover During Monsoon: Sentinel-2 optical passes may be obscured. Mitigation: Automated cloud-masking triggers targeted low-altitude UAV sorties and weather-driven epidemiological forecasting that operates continuously even during cloudy spells.
- Sparse Historical Pest Labels: Departmental outbreak logs may be incomplete. Mitigation: Combine mechanistic AAU/NIBSM pest-biology thresholds with semi-supervised anomaly detection and structured field-officer ground-truthing during Phase 2–4.
- Last-Mile Farmer Adoption & Alert Fatigue: Generic or overly frequent alerts erode trust. Mitigation: Enforce human-in-the-loop verification before broadcast and deliver concise, IPM-first advisories in Assamese, Bengali, and Bodo.

Pilot Validation Plan: Upon MVP readiness (Week 16), we propose a 6-to-8-week controlled pilot across 3–4 representative agricultural blocks selected with ASSAC and the Department of Agriculture (covering Sali/Boro Paddy, Mustard/Potato, Tea, and Horticulture). Pilot blocks will be paired with control scouting zones to measure early-warning lead time, alert precision/recall, officer verification turnaround, and chemical input reduction—providing empirical evidence for state-wide replication across Assam's districts.
